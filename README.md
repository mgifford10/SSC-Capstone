# SSC-Capstone
How a child's upbringing and familial factors influence political party affiliation
This study explores the relationship between childhood upbringing and political affiliation, emphasizing socioeconomic status, geographic stability, and parental education amongst other factors. By examining the research question, “In what ways does a child’s upbringing, including factors like socioeconomic status, geographic stability, family size, religion, and parental education levels, impact their political party affiliation?”, this study combines quantitative and qualitative methods to provide a comprehensive analysis. The quantitative analysis utilizes data from the GSS Data Explorer, focusing on variables such as “race,” “mobile16” (geographic mobility since age 16),  “generations”: (amount of generations per household), “relig”: (religion raised in), “maeduc” (maternal education), and “paeduc” (paternal education), with “polviews” (political views) as the outcome variable. T-tests and multivariate regression analyses reveal significant associations between political views and parental education, geographic stability, and racial background. Notably, higher maternal and paternal education levels correlate with more liberal views, while geographic stability tends toward conservatism. Although the models’ explanatory power is limited, these findings highlight subtle but meaningful influences of childhood environment on political perspectives. Academic articles and qualitative research will further complement these results, offering nuanced insights into family discussions, socioeconomic factors, and cultural contexts during upbringing. This research contributes to understanding how early-life experiences and environmental factors shape political ideologies, demonstrating the importance of multifaceted approaches in political socialization studies.
This study employs a mixed-methods approach, integrating quantitative analysis of General Social Survey (GSS) data with qualitative insights from existing literature. The primary methodological framework is multivariate regression analysis, which allows for an examination of the relationship between childhood upbringing variables and political affiliation. By utilizing the GSS dataset, this study aims to assess the extent to which socioeconomic status, geographic stability, parental education, and religious background influence an individual's ideological orientation.
